## 5. Test network, coins and explorers

- **Test BTC:** https://wallet.surge.dev ("SurgeSats", a keyless MPC wallet on Surge's signet; sign-in by passkey or Google). It is a source of coins only and cannot act as a signer. It works for the human only once signed in, so before the first funding request ask them to open it and confirm they see a balance; never create an account or sign in there yourself. Coins move only through the funding protocol in §8.3.
- **Test USDC:** Base Sepolia. There is no reliable faucet; the harness asks the human through the same protocol. Borrowed USDC arrives in each pair's EVM address on its own; extra USDC covers interest on a full repay and the `INSUFFICIENT_USDC_BALANCE` cases. Ten USDC, sent to the one identity that repays in full, covered a whole run. **(verified)**
- **Explorers:** Bitcoin signet https://signet.surge.dev (esplora API https://esplora.signet.surge.dev), Base Sepolia https://sepolia.basescan.org; mainnet https://mempool.space and https://basescan.org.
- **Protocol truth:** https://testnet.earn.surge.credit/#/vaults, with the caveats of §4.4. Label markets by terms, never by ID.
- **Mainnet funds** (BTC and USDC on Base) are bought externally by the human and only used in the gated step of §8.6.

## 8. The test harness: identities, funding, timing and levels

**8.1 Test wallet.** On first run the harness generates its own BIP-39 mnemonic (256 bits from `crypto.randomBytes`), writes it to `.e2e/wallet.json` with mode 0600 (git-ignored from the first commit) and prints only derived addresses. If `TEST_MNEMONIC` is set, that phrase is used and the file records only a fingerprint. The README explains how to back the file up and that losing it strands any coins held by the identities. The mainnet paid run uses a separate `.e2e/wallet.mainnet.json`, generated the same way and funded by the human with exactly the approved amounts.

**8.2 Identities.** Identity N is EVM `m/44'/60'/N'/0/0` and BTC `m/86'/1'/N'/0/0` on signet. A fresh index is a fresh pair, a fresh vault and a fresh relayer profile, which §4.7 requires per lifecycle. Roles: 0 funds, 1 is parked, 2 and up are lifecycles, and one more is the human's own UI session (§8.7). Keep a `.e2e/identities.json` ledger of which index did what, with balances and funding txids. **Identity 0 is the funding wallet:** its own P2TR address holds the signet BTC the human sends; E2E runs fund vaults from it with their own key-path transactions through the esplora API, and withdrawals pay back to it, so one funding lasts. **Open a parked position with identity 1 on day one and never touch it**, so that about 38 days later K2, H5 and L4-overdue become live; record its deadline block and date.

**8.3 Funding protocol (human in the loop).** (1) Pre-flight: compute the run's need from `getRequiredCollateral` at the live price plus fees, add 50%, read balances on chain, ask only for the shortfall. (2) Print one funding request block and stop until the human says it is sent: asset, network, destination, amount, purpose, explorer link; batch so the human is interrupted as few times as possible. (3) The first BTC request is preceded by the SurgeSats sign-in check of §5. (4) USDC goes to the identity that repays in full; the others need none unless they repay. (5) After the human confirms, poll the chain until confirmed (one confirmation for BTC, the receipt for USDC), record the txids, then continue. Never proceed on the human's word alone. (6) Recycle: withdrawals pay back to identity 0; the ledger makes later runs ask for less. **What a run costs (verified):** at the signet oracle's $190,000, a $5 line needs 3,339 sats on the 80% market and 5,342 on the 50% market; three lifecycles with every top-up, withdrawal and renewal fee consumed about **30,000 sats and under 10 USDC in total**. Ask for about 100,000 sats and 10 USDC; never millions.

**8.4 Live-read fixtures** (verified 2026-10-29; re-verify before relying on them). Signet: #1 (in liquidation, past exit), #34 (inactive with collateral), #1361 (overdue, dust debt), #1982 (547-sat residue), #1987 (no debt, past exit), #2018 (liquidated then re-collateralised), #2130 (dust debt), #2194 (LTV 78.8% on an 80% market), #2200 (fixed market at max LTV), #2201 (swept, still active), #2202 (parked), #2203 (fixed market, $5 debt). Mainnet: #1, #2, #55, #56 (legacy, `marketId: null`), #112 (liquidated then re-collateralised), #185–#187, #190, #195, #200, #204 (fully liquidated, still `isActive`).

**8.5 Timing on signet (verified).** Blocks about every 64 s; 3 confirmations to mint; mint 4 to 8 minutes after the send; sync, withdrawal and renewal 4 to 9 minutes to finalize; `sign-refresh` 400s for about 10 s; the dashboard about 20 minutes behind. The E2E runner is resumable: progress per identity in a state file; a failure part-way does not mean re-funding; one-shot steps are marked before they run; `--reset` clears, `--status` prints.

**8.6 Test levels and what each must prove.**

1. **Static:** strict type check, lint, a secret scanner over the repo and the built bundle (JWTs, PSBTs, xprv, PEM, mnemonic word runs; allowlist the SDK's public anon keys), and the build-time assertion that the test-signer injection point, the harness wallet code and the `/dev` entry are absent from production output and the Buffer polyfill is present.
2. **Unit:** formatting (BTC and sats, USD strings), scaled-integer comparison at boundaries, LTV and health derivation, end-state derivation (§4.16), countdown maths from block height and measured rate, the error contract (every code present), rejection normalisation including message-text matching, the boot and resume state machine (every combination of pending deposit, pending or stuck withdrawal, pending or stuck extension, sync in flight, no-op sync), `isFlowTerminal` versus `classifyStatus`, the chain gate, the typed-data recorder, the signer adapters against fixtures, the prompt counts per flow (§3.9).
3. **Component:** every screen against a mocked session, including each error state, each blocked-button state, prompt-progress rendering, screens staying mounted through a reboot, and watcher cleanup.
4. **SDK contract against a mocked relayer:** intercept the gateway and the EVM RPC (dispatch `eth_call` by selector with ABI encoding), replay recorded responses captured from real signet runs (JWTs scrubbed), mint sessions with `setDebugAccessToken`, and exercise the real SDK through the service layer so the mapping from relayer code and status to `BorrowError.code` is tested for all 29 codes, including the ones that cannot be provoked live (`RELAYER_OUT_OF_GAS`, `RELAYER_UNAVAILABLE`, `IDENTITY_CONFLICT`, `NONCE_REUSED`, `SIGN_REQUEST_EXPIRED`, `WITHDRAWAL_AMOUNT_MISMATCH`, `POSITION_IN_LIQUIDATION`), plus the resume classifier for every recorded state.
5. **Live reads** (no wallet, both networks, nightly): markets, price, fee rates and the fallback detection, block height and rate, the fixtures of §8.4, the on-chain `DOMAIN_SEPARATOR()` on both chains. Assert shapes and invariants, not values.
6. **E2E against live signet with real coins**, driving the real product UI with the harness identities. Two ways to get the keys in, in order of preference: (a) a test build imports the harness identity's seed through the product's ordinary restore flow and the test drives the product's own confirmation sheets, so the production signing path is exercised; (b) where the product cannot be seeded under test, a signer bridge: the test process holds the keys and exposes `signMessage` / `signTypedData` / `signPsbt` to the app through an injection point that exists only in test builds (a flag-guarded handle on the app controller, adopted on every page load), with controls to reject or delay the next signature of a kind and to record typed-data payloads; private keys never enter the app process. Playwright on web; Detox or Maestro on React Native; the platform's integration driver on Flutter or native; the extension's own harness for extensions. Pre-flight first (§8.3). Then the lifecycle on a fresh identity: connect → login → register → verify vault → open a minimum line on the variable market → fund from identity 0 → mint → USDC landed → add collateral sized for ≥ $5 of headroom → draw within headroom → draw above headroom (routed to `borrowMoreSync`) → partial repay → full repay with margin → switch to the fixed market → draw → repay → partial withdraw → interrupted withdraw (reject the Bitcoin signature) → resume → proactive renewal → interrupted renewal → resume → wait for the extension gate to clear → full sweep → end-state reads → attempt a new deposit and record `LOAN_ALREADY_ACTIVE`, with a restart of the app at every † point. Then the fixed-market run on another fresh identity: underfund → observe `failed` → fresh deposit on the pre-funded vault → mint with surplus → J11 on the resulting low-debt position. Then the negative suites: every pre-signature refusal (D4, G3, G4, J5, J6, J14, G5) with zero prompts, and the session cases (B3, B7, A5). Record on-chain evidence per step and render it into `docs/E2E-REPORT.md`. **Mainnet paid run:** only if the human approves in writing after reading a dry-run plan with the exact amounts and fees; minimum borrow, smallest collateral the market allows, partial withdraw and sweep, and nothing else, on `.e2e/wallet.mainnet.json`.
7. **Manual QA** only for what the platform's automation cannot reach (biometric prompts, OS permission sheets, a real phone); with software keys nothing else is manual. Record results with redacted screenshots in the E2E report.

**8.7 A session for the human without extensions.** Give the harness a "drive" command: it opens the product (a real browser window for web, the app on a simulator otherwise) signed by an unused identity's bridged signers, confirms every signature in the terminal first (Enter signs, `n` rejects, which is how a person reaches the stuck states), prints the identity's addresses and the vault, and pairs with a `pay <address> <sats>` command that funds a vault from identity 0. For a web product it starts the dev server itself with the test flag and a non-throttling RPC, refuses at once a server that was started without the flag (the injection point is folded into the client bundle), and sets the RPC through the product's own endpoint override so reads never go through the SDK's default. This is how a reviewer who installs no wallet extension clicks through every flow.

CI runs levels 1 to 4 on every push and level 5 nightly; level 6 runs on demand with the funded state file.
